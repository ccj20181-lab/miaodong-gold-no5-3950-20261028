Weekend Gold Update

October 25, 2026

AG Thorson, CMT
Oct 25, 2026

Last year, gold rallied from around $3,400 in September to over $5,600 by January, and bullish sentiment was through the roof.

Fast-forward nine months, and now some investors are questioning whether the bull market is over. This is exactly how markets work, and precisely the type of sentiment that often accompanies turning points.

Looking at the bigger picture, I remain confident that gold could reach $10,000 and silver $300 by around 2030. The current environment shows how quickly sentiment can shift, and nine months from now, investors could once again chase higher gold prices as they surge $100 or more per day.

The Gold Cycle Indicator finished at 25. We are officially in the lower quartile of the GCI (deeply oversold).

10-YEAR YIELDS: The 10-year Treasury yield finished the week just below 5.00%. Next week’s Fed decision and PCE inflation data could prove pivotal. Another weekly close below 5.00% would strengthen the case for an October peak, while a move back above 5.10% could signal further upside.

WTIC: Crude oil is dangerously close to breaking above trendline resistance near $105. A sustained breakout could trigger another surge toward $120, potentially wreaking havoc across financial markets.

GOLD: This time last year, gold was surging $50 to $100 per day. Now, everyone is wondering if prices will ever stop falling. This is how markets work, and precisely the type of sentiment that often accompanies a durable bottom.

We could see a brief capitulation if gold slips below $3,950, which I suspect would mark the final washout.

The only scenario where I see gold falling toward $3,500 is if we experience a severe stock market decline following the midterm elections. Even then, I would expect a durable bottom to form before year-end.

SILVER: Silver finished the week below $55.00, making a test of trendline support near $50.00 increasingly likely. In a worst-case scenario, if gold were to fall toward $3,500 amid a sharp stock market selloff, silver could briefly retreat to the $40.00–$45.00 range.

For now, however, I continue to look for a bottom to form between now and the first week or two of November.

PLATINUM: Platinum is testing the midyear lows, and a retest of the $1,500 support level is likely before bottoming.

GDX: Miners continue to break down, with the next support level near $78.00. It would take a sustained decline in gold below $3,850 to increase the likelihood of miners retesting their July lows.

GDXJ: Junior miners finished the week below $100, raising the possibility of a retest of the July low over the next week or two. If prices find support near that level, it could set the stage for a potential double bottom.

SILJ: Silver juniors broke the July low. If silver continues to slide toward $50.00, SILJ could potentially retest the $20.00 - $21.00 support zone.

NEM: Newmont continues to have one of the strongest-looking charts in the sector, and I’m closely monitoring it for signs of a bottom. Prices finished the week just below the 200-day moving average, with the next significant support level near $105.

S&P 500: The S&P 500 closed progressively below its 50-day EMA, increasing the risk of an intermediate decline. A break below the September low at 7,507 would open the door to an immediate retest of the 200-day moving average. If that support fails, a swift decline toward 7,000 becomes increasingly likely.

Unfortunately, a broad equity selloff could drag other markets lower, including gold and precious metals. However, I would expect any resulting weakness in metals to be relatively brief.

BITCOIN: Bitcoin continues to roll over in a pattern similar to May. A sustained breakdown below $80,000 could trigger additional selling and an immediate test of $75,000. If that support fails, prices could decline sharply. A lower low in Q4 remains a possibility, though I suspect it would require a 15% to 20% correction in the S&P 500.

Next week, I’ll be closely monitoring crude oil and the market’s reaction to Wednesday’s Fed decision and Thursday’s PCE inflation report.

Have a great week.
